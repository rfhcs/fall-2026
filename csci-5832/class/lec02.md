## English inflection

- -s indicates a plural noun
- -ed, -ing, -s indicates a plural verb

and that's basically it for english inflections

## What is derivational morphology?

things that are not taught well to second-speakers of english.

- affixes such as:
    - -al, -tion, -ee, -er, -ant, -ness
    - anti-, un-, non-

"quasi-systematic" = not really a rule

### How do we change a noun/verb to an adjective?

using affixes such as: -al, -able, -ive, -less

## How do we classify languages?

- genetically: family history (romance lang, germanic, etc.)
- areal: geographical occurence
- typological: shares grammar, morphology, phonology, syntax, etc.

morphological typology groups languages based on morphology

#### isolating vs. synthetic

- a spectrum
- isolating = one morpheme per word
- synthetic = lots of morphemes per words

## What is computational morphology?

- takes surface form and returns the set of morphological features
- English + Chinese makes this easy b/c these languages have simple morphemes

### How hard is it to find morphemes in a word?

#### agglutinating vs. fusional

- agglutinating = concat stems w/ affixes to form words
- fusional = form changes based on the morpheme

## What is a vocabulary?

it's the set of words that a system has access to

**questions to consider**

- what should a vocabulary contain?
- all words in a language?
- a subset?
    - how many?
    - what words to include/exclude?
    - can we add/remove any of them?

## What is a corpora?

a currated list of words designed for a specific application

### what are the dimensions?

- language
- provenance: where was it obtained?
- genere: fiction, non-fiction, etc.
- demographics: gender, writer's age, etc.
- medium: spoken, audio, captioned, video, etc.
- writeing system: 350 systems in active use

## Heap's Law

$$
|V| = kN^{\beta}
$$

the size of a vocabulary grows somewhat faster than the square root of the corpus size

- there is no tail and it doesn't flatten
